# Top 10 findings (data through Oct 21, 2026, 8:38 PM PT)

> Written from data through Oct 21. Final totals through Oct 23 (project end) are in `FINAL_SUMMARY.md`.

> **Terms:** a *session* is one connection to the fake SSH/Telnet login (Cowrie). A *credential* is the username/password a bot tried. An *ASN* is the network operator that owns an IP. *HASSH* is a fingerprint of an SSH client's settings. More terms are in `GLOSSARY.md`.

Source: the SQLite index built from this repo's logs (5,409,998 events, read-only; see `dashboard/build_stats.py`). Cowrie window: Oct 14, 11:23 PM PT to Oct 21, 8:38 PM PT.

1. **One Telnet botnet makes up most of the traffic.** Of 233,760 Cowrie sessions, 216,710 (93%) were Telnet and only 17,050 were SSH, but the number of IPs was about the same on each (544 vs 563). Telnet logins succeeded 190,198 times. Telnet bots are few, and each one retries tirelessly.
2. **A few residential IPs in Pakistan and China do most of it.** By sessions, Pakistan leads with 120,133 and China follows with 72,803, while the US has only 2,485. The single busiest IP, 182.115.160.251 (China Unicom), opened 35,143 sessions. Next come 124.29.251.150 with 20,949 and 124.29.221.223 with 17,246, both on Cyber Internet Services in Pakistan.
3. **The "ximo" with blank password campaign.** `ximo` with an empty password is the top credential, used 31,172 times by 29 IPs. 19 of those IPs are in Pakistan (16 on Cyber Internet Services) and 8 are on China Unicom, which is the same cluster as finding 2.
4. **The credentials are IoT and router factory defaults, not human passwords.** The top pairs are root/xc3511 (10,915, a Xiongmai DVR default), root/Zte521 (8,693), root/samsung (7,391), draytek/1234 (5,298), 8ehomeasb (5,150), cuadmin/cu@Hnunicom (3,312, China Unicom routers), admin/CenturyL1nk (2,708) and root/7ujMko0admin (1,426, a known Mirai default). admin/admin came from the most IPs, 219.
5. **Mirai-style droppers were scripted the same way thousands of times.** The most common command chain (`start; enable; config terminal; system; linuxshell; su; sh; ...cd to a writable dir; /bin/busybox ...`) ran 32,870 times from a single IP. Variants of it fill nearly all of the top 25 chains. Bots wrote an ARM ELF header with `echo -e '\x7F\x45\x4C\x46...'` and ran `/bin/busybox HISILICON`, which targets HiSilicon camera chipsets.
6. **There were 106,944 malware downloads but only 70 unique files.** Nearly all of them fetched a file named `i` from high ports on the attackers' own IPs, such as http://123.233.198.83:52155/i (6,716 times). That points to infected devices serving the payload to the next victim (peer spreading). Three hashes dominate: 12013662… (55,949 + 913 downloads, 23 IPs), 4293c1d8… (25,255 + 830, 20 IPs) and f6c97b1e… (16,117 + 269, 12 IPs). Another 32,951 downloads failed.
7. **The "mdrfckr" SSH key backdoor.** 69 IPs ran `rm -rf .ssh && mkdir .ssh && echo "ssh-rsa AAAAB3...ArDp4cun... mdrfckr" >> .ssh/authorized_keys`, 88 times in total. The written file has hash a8460f44…, and that hash was logged from exactly the same 69 IPs. The top countries were China (16), US (9) and Vietnam (6), and the IPs sit mostly on cloud hosts (Volcano Engine, UCloud, Oracle, Baidu). This is a different group from the Telnet botnet.
8. **SSH is a Go scanner.** 15,485 of 16,094 SSH client banners (96%) were `SSH-2.0-Go`, from 147 IPs: 49 in the US, 39 in the Netherlands, 24 on TechTies and 15 on Censys. One HASSH fingerprint, 0a07365c…, covered 14,334 key exchanges from just 24 IPs.
9. **Volume grew every day, and most IPs show up only once.** Daily sessions went 10,337 (Oct 15) → 23,897 (Oct 18) → 46,768 (Oct 19) → 66,626 (Oct 20) → 72,107 (Oct 21, partial day). Of 1,021 Cowrie IPs, 896 (88%) came on only one day, and only 2 came on all 8 days: 50.2.184.242 (Eonix) and 20.39.42.109 (Microsoft). Sessions are short, with a median of 5.0 s and 99% under 181 s.
10. **OpenCanary shows what Cowrie can't, and the scanners are mostly cloud.** Redis on port 6379 got 517,487 hits from 348 IPs, with 156 of those IPs on one ASN, AO ALMAZ. Next came VNC on 5900 (201,260 hits; Tencent and Alibaba lead) and RDP on 3389 (476 IPs, the most of any port, led by Google and Microsoft). Only 233 IPs hit both OpenCanary and Cowrie, out of 2,059 and 1,021.

Bonus: time of day. Cowrie sessions peak 7 to 9 PM PT (8 PM: 12,673) and bottom out at 8 to 10 AM PT (9 AM: 5,029).

## Method and caveats
- Counts come from a read-only SQLite index of the raw logs. Cowrie counts come from the eventid inside each raw record. Times were converted from utc_time to PT.
- **Gaps:** Oct 16 (4,954) and Oct 17 (8,920) are low because Cowrie recorded nothing from Oct 16 ~10:20 AM to Oct 17 ~4 PM PT (an outage of about 30 hours). See `DATA_GAPS_AND_ISSUES.md`. Oct 21 ends at 8:38 PM PT.
- Geo and ASN data come from offline DB-IP Lite. 1,334 of 2,899 IPs have no Cloud/Home tag. Country is by IP and doesn't mean the attacker is there.
- Cowrie accepts most logins (205,666 succeeded vs 7,507 failed), so "successful logins" means the bot got in to Cowrie, not to a real device. Commands and downloads were captured by the emulator. Nothing was unpacked.
- "Command chain" means all inputs in a session joined together, and the top chains differ only by the target folder or payload. Malware types were not checked against VirusTotal. Labels such as Mirai or Xiongmai are inferred from public default creds and the HISILICON/busybox pattern.
- The first Cowrie session (Oct 14, 11:23 PM PT) came from 172.58.210.229 (T-Mobile US) and lasted 99 ms. It is possibly test traffic from the operator. The pre-Cowrie snapshot (2,324 events, 96 IPs) also includes what looks like possible test traffic from AWS (32.192.139.229).
